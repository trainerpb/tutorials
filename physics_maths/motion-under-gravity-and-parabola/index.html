Why We Learn By Use-Case: The 4 Pillars of Mastery

Everything connects when you look deep enough

1. Build From the Foundation Up (Zero Rote Memorization)

Never treat a formula like a magic spell dropped from the sky. Ask: Where did y² = 4ax come from? It starts from a simple geometric promise—a point moving so its distance from a fixed point (Focus) equals its perpendicular distance from a fixed line (Directrix), giving eccentricity e = PS / PM = 1. When your foundation is rock-solid, even the toughest twisted problem—like a chewing gum stuck to a rolling wheel or a projectile launched from a moving platform—becomes a natural extension of basics.

2. Teach Every Subject by Use-Case: Rohit's Sixer!

Why teach Coordinate Geometry in a math period and Projectile Kinematics two months later in physics as if they are strangers? When Rohit Sharma ("The Hitman") leans into a pull shot and sends the cricket ball soaring into the stands, universe doesn't pause to switch textbooks! The Grand Unification: Gravity pulls vertically (y = u_y·t - ½gt²) while horizontal velocity stays steady (x = u_x·t). Eliminate time t, and boom—Newton's Mechanics reveals Apollonius's Conic Parabola (y = x·tanθ - gx² / 2u²cos²θ)! The highest point of Rohit's sixer is the Vertex of the parabola; the latus rectum governs the flatness of the arc!

3. Code What You Learn: Turn Equations into Living Worlds

If you can code it, you truly understand it. Static blackboard diagrams freeze motion, hiding the intuition. By translating equations into HTML5 Canvas simulations, Python scripts, or interactive sliders, you can tweak launch velocity u, angle θ, or gravity g in real time and watch the directrix, focus, velocity vectors, and locus evolve before your eyes. Coding transforms passive students into active scientific creators.

4. Brainstorm, Correlate & Apply Beyond the Classroom

What happens when a chewing gum sticks to the rim of a bicycle wheel? Or when a cricketer catches a ball near the boundary rope? Or how satellite dishes focus weak radio signals onto a single receiver using the reflection property of a parabola? Soham Sir challenges you to look at everyday puzzles ("ChewingGum Problems"), strip away the noise, correlate mechanics with geometry, and brainstorm multiple elegant solutions.
